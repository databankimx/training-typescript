# Ch04 · Numbers

Numbers are the values most programs are really about. Prices, scores, and counts all need careful handling, and the way computers store decimals has a few habits that will surprise you. This chapter covers the common ones, so the surprises at least arrive expected.

Numbers are the values you calculate with: prices, scores, counts, and measurements. This chapter shows what a number can be, how to round it and display it, how computers store decimal numbers and where that causes surprises, and how to read numbers from text.

Numbers have their own type, `number`. The checker treats them differently from text: you can add, subtract, and compare numbers, but the checker stops you from doing those things to text, unless the text is first converted.

## What you'll learn

1. What a number is, and how to check whether a value is a whole number.
2. How to round a number, and how to show it with a fixed number of decimal places.
3. Why some decimal sums are slightly wrong, and how to avoid the problem with money.
4. How numbers can be written in binary and hexadecimal.
5. How to turn text into a number, and what happens when the text isn't a number.
6. The `Math` library, which does the common calculations for you.

The chapter ends with a summary, and two homework exercises.
